Managing the crisis is not the same as solving it.

On 26 August 2026 a mountainside of rock and ice on Tsangbu Ri fell into the Lende Khola and became a flood that killed or left missing more than 6,700 people along the Trishuli. This site puts the evidence in one place: where the ice is, how the gorges funnel water, what seismometers heard, and who lives in the path.

01 · THE ICE

glaciers

Nepal holds about 4,500 glaciers covering km². Most of the ice sits between 5,000 and 6,000 m, the band that is warming fastest. Beneath them, glacial lakes now cover 8% more area than in 2016–17.

02 · ACROSS THE BORDER

46% of the ice is abroad

Much of the ice feeding Nepal's rivers lies in Tibet: 62% for the Koshi basin, which also holds 42 of the 47 potentially dangerous lakes, 25 of them in China. The hazard starts outside Nepal's jurisdiction. So must the warning.

03 · THE FUNNELS

km of gorges

We cut ~24,000 cross-sections across Nepal's rivers on a 30 m elevation model. Where both valley walls rise 400 m within a kilometre of the river, we call it a gorge. Within 100 km downstream of ice, 46% of river length is gorge, against 9% elsewhere. Floods cannot spread out there; they stack up.

04 · 26 AUGUST 2026

8 minutes to the border

At 08:37 NPT about 100 million m³ of rock and ice broke from Tsangbu Ri. The flood reached Gyirong Port in ~7 minutes and ran through gorges for 51 of its first 61 km. It damaged 12 hydropower projects and buried bazaars built on the valley floor.

06 · THE SIGNALS WERE THERE

Heard in seconds

Earthquakes strike the whole length of Nepal: the west (337 M≥4 since 1900) as often as the east. Seismometers also hear mountains fall. The 2026 collapse registered at 110× normal shaking 57 km away within a minute, and a small ML 2.5 event 17 minutes earlier is visible in the same open record.

07 · PEOPLE IN THE PATH

people

That many people live on valley floors within 100 km below glacier ice; 600,000 live along glacier-fed rivers. We flag settlements whose position, upstream ice, dangerous lakes and gorges put them in the path of a cascade.

08 · WE KEEP BUILDING IN THE PATH

× more buildings

Built-up area on glacier-fed valley floors grew 5.7-fold between 1975 and 2025 (GHSL), faster than Nepal as a whole (4.5×). It grew 7.7× in the Trishuli corridor and 9.4× along the Bhotekoshi–Sunkoshi. km of road run along these floors.

26 August 2026, minute by minute

Open seismic data from Kakani (NK.KKN, 57 km away) show the precursor at −17 min, the collapse at 08:37, and a second "flood rumble" peaking ~41 minutes later as the flood passed the lower Trishuli. The bars show when the flood front would reach each place for front speeds from 52 m/s (the measured peak in the first 25 km) down to 20 m/s. A seismic alert at the moment of collapse would have left the light-blue minutes to act.

Who is exposed, and who can least cope?

Exposure: people living on valley floors within 100 km below glacier ice (WorldPop 2020, scaled to Census 2021). Vulnerability: a transparent score averaging four percentile ranks: share of children under 5, share of adults 65+, share of the district more than 60 minutes from a hospital (HeiGIT), and provincial multidimensional poverty (DHS 2022).

Method in brief

Rivers: D8 routing on the 90 m Copernicus DEM. Gorges: perpendicular cross-sections on the 30 m DEM. Exposure: height above nearest drainage ≤20 m. Seismic: open FDSN waveforms (NK.KKN, IO.EVN, GFZ XN) and the USGS, EMSC and ISC catalogues. Literature: Adhikari et al. 2026 (Seismica); Agarwala 2026 (NBSEH); Cook et al. 2018, 2021; ICIMOD/UNDP 2020.

This is a screening tool. It is not an official hazard map or warning service. In an emergency follow NDRRMA and DHM.
